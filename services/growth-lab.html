The Problem

Running Tactics Is Not The Same As Running Experiments.

Most teams don’t have an ideas problem. They have a testing problem: too many ideas, no agreed order, slow cycles, and wins that never get documented or repeated. Output goes up in places that don’t compound, and every quarter starts from a fresh guess.

What Growth Lab Is

An Ongoing Operating Layer For Growth, Not A Campaign.

Growth Lab™ sits on top of the GTM you already run. It watches the funnel for the next opportunity, prioritizes it against everything else that could be tested, runs the test, reads the outcome, and scales or stops it. The work is continuous by design — the next cycle starts from what the last cycle proved.

The Growth Experimentation Loop

Observe → Hypothesize → Prioritize → Build → Launch → Measure → Learn → Repeat

Eight stages, one repeating cycle. Each stage has an input, an output and an owner, and each output is the next stage’s input. The loop — not the campaign — is the unit of work.

01

Observe

Funnel performance, buyer behaviour, sales feedback and win/loss input read together — not one dashboard in isolation.

Out: candidate opportunities

02

Hypothesize

Each opportunity becomes a written belief about what is holding the funnel, and the evidence behind it.

Out: a claim that can be proven wrong

03

Prioritize

Impact, confidence, effort and fit decide the order. Everything else waits in the backlog with its reason recorded.

Out: one agreed test order

04

Build

The variant, asset, page, sequence or workflow change — plus the tracking needed to read it honestly.

Out: a test that can be read

05

Launch

Shipped with the audience, timing and instrumentation the test needs — not launched and hoped over.

Out: a live test

06

Measure

Read against the signal agreed before launch, over a window long enough for the result to mean anything.

Out: evidence, not opinion

07

Learn

The belief held, weakened or broke. Recorded with what we would do differently next time.

Out: a written learning

08

Repeat

What is proven becomes standard, and the next cycle starts from the new evidence rather than a new guess.

Out: a stronger next cycle

repeat

Repeat is not a phase that ends — it is the behaviour the whole lab is built around. Each cycle should make the next one cheaper to run, faster to read and better informed. That is what separates a growth system from a quarter of activity.

How We Prioritize Experiments

Test What Could Matter. Not What Is Easiest To Launch.

Every candidate experiment answers the same four questions before it takes capacity. The answers get written down, so the reasoning is visible, arguable and comparable from one cycle to the next.

trending_up

Potential Impact

If this works, how much could it move the number it is connected to? A test on the constraint beats a test on something that is already performing.

psychology

Confidence

What evidence already exists? Past results, funnel behaviour, sales feedback and win/loss input all count. A hunch counts less — and it is labelled as one.

build

Effort

What does it take to build, launch and read properly — in days, in people and in risk? A test that cannot be read cleanly is not worth running at all.

account_tree

Strategic Fit

Does this move the GTM you are executing this year, or the one you were executing two years ago? Alignment decides whether a win is worth repeating.

Impact against effort

Two of the four questions decide where a candidate sits on the board. The other two decide how it is run and how much it is trusted.

Potential impact

High impact · Low effort

Run these immediately, while the bigger bets are still being built. This is where compounding starts.

Strategic Bets

Fund deliberately and sequence carefully. These are the moves the next cycle is built around.

Fill-Ins

Worth doing only when they are nearly free. Never a priority, never on the critical path.

Reconsider

Where good teams quietly lose a quarter. Usually a stop rather than a project.

Four questions, not a proprietary formula — there is no hidden scoring model and no weighted index behind this. The order is agreed with your team, and it changes as the evidence changes.

FAQ

B2B Growth Experimentation — Questions, Answered

Straight answers on what Growth Lab™ is, how experiments get prioritized, how results are judged, and what happens when one wins.

What is a GTM Growth Lab? expand_more

A GTM Growth Lab is an ongoing operating layer for growth rather than a campaign. It continuously looks across the funnel for the next opportunity worth testing, turns it into a written hypothesis, runs the test, reads the result against an agreed signal, and turns what worked into a repeatable GTM system. In DreamGTM’s Growth Lab™ that cycle runs inside the DreamGTM Method™ — Discover, Design, Execute, Optimize, Scale — instead of restarting from a fresh guess every quarter.

How is Growth Lab different from an agency? expand_more

An agency produces activity on a schedule: a campaign, a content calendar, a set of assets. Growth Lab runs an experimentation process. Every piece of work starts as a hypothesis with a defined signal, is tested against what was believed before, and ends in a decision — scale it, iterate it, or stop it. We do not sell campaigns, guaranteed results or a fixed volume of tests.

What types of experiments do you run? expand_more

Experiments run across the areas DreamGTM already builds and runs: positioning and messaging, website and landing pages, conversion rate optimization, content and SEO, demand generation, outbound and pipeline, lifecycle and nurture, AI and automation, and sales enablement. The mix is chosen from your funnel evidence rather than from a fixed package — the areas where a change could move a number for you.

How are experiments prioritized? expand_more

Four questions, agreed with your team and asked of every candidate test: what is the potential impact if it works, how confident are we that it will, what will it cost to run, and does it fit the strategy you are actually executing. There is no proprietary score and no black box — the reasoning is written down, the order is agreed before anything launches, and it changes as the evidence changes.

How do you measure success? expand_more

Against the signal agreed before the test starts, and against the commercial outcome it feeds. Reach, engagement depth and conversion are diagnostics; the report is about what moved the number — qualified pipeline, conversion between funnel stages — and which lever to fund next. Where the data does not exist yet to judge a test, we say so before running it rather than reporting a result that means nothing.

How quickly can experiments launch? expand_more

It depends on the test, and we will be specific in the first conversation. A landing page or message variant usually moves faster than an audience, pricing or positioning change, and anything needing new data, tracking or a new asset takes longer to set up cleanly. We do not commit to launch windows in advance, because the honest answer depends on your stack, your approvals and what the test needs.

What happens when an experiment wins? expand_more

It stops being an experiment and becomes system. The winning approach is standardized into the asset, workflow or playbook it belongs to, rolled out across the segments where it applies, and monitored so the standard holds. Growth Lab™ is where experiments are found and proven; once something works it moves into Demand Flow™ or Pipeline Acceleration™ to be run at scale, with AI-Native GTM™ automating the workflow layer underneath it.

Can Growth Lab work with an existing marketing team? expand_more

Yes. The work runs alongside your team rather than around it — your team brings the context and the judgement, we bring the process, the experiment design and the execution load. The backlog, hypotheses, test plans and learnings are documented and shared, so the capability stays in the business instead of staying with an agency.
